# Wishlist → purchase: within 30 days and overall

Help more people buy at least one saved item — both eventually (wishlist → purchase in general) and within a month of saving it. Not a star-rating summary.

These answers cover two horizons: what stops a saved item from becoming an order at all, and what pushes the buy past a month — not star ratings.

## Scenario · Wishlist → purchase (in general)

Wishlist → purchase in general fails when shoppers hit blockers after saving — price, delivery or checkout of a saved item, and sale timing. This is about ever converting a saved item, not only the 30-day clock.

- Users wait for a sale or price drop before buying saved items because they are waiting for sale timing (for example EORS). — This often stops people from buying a saved item at all
- Users wait for a sale or price drop before buying saved items because the current price feels too high. — This often stops people from buying a saved item at all

### How do these behaviors differ across user segments?

*Horizon: Both horizons*

Wishlist hesitation is not one behavior — it splits by shopper type, not by phone or app version. We only name a segment when shoppers use explicit cues (sale wait, salary talk, wedding mention, etc.); shares below count comments with that cue, not every wishlist user. Price-first savers (Deal hunters / sale shoppers and Budget / salary-cycle shoppers — about 11.2% of these comments): the list works like a deal tracker. They plan to buy, but only after EORS, a price drop, or payday — the delay is timing or price, not loss of interest. Loyal repeat shoppers (9.4%): they already order on Myntra but still park items. Being a regular customer does not mean saved items convert quickly. Still-building-trust shoppers (First-time shoppers — about 3.4%): new or cautious buyers save while they learn fit, quality, and whether products match photos. The wishlist is a shortlist for research, not a committed buy list. Occasion-led shoppers (Office / workwear shoppers and Wedding / occasion shoppers — about 3.4%): they save outfits for weddings, office, or festivals and pause until the look fits the moment — styling confidence gates the buy more than price. Gen Z, wedding, and festive cues are thinner in public wishlist talk (Office / workwear shoppers, Budget / salary-cycle shoppers, and Wedding / occasion shoppers); treat those shares as directional and validate with survey quotas.

- Repeat / loyal shoppers: 9.4% (n=25)
- First-time shoppers: 3.4% (n=9)
- Office / workwear shoppers: 1.9% (n=5)
- Budget / salary-cycle shoppers: 1.5% (n=4)
- Wedding / occasion shoppers: 1.5% (n=4)

### What unmet needs emerge consistently across user conversations?

*Horizon: Both horizons*

Across user conversations, the unmet needs that emerge most consistently are gaps shoppers still need filled after they save an item — not restating the delay reasons themselves. The strongest repeating needs are: a price they feel is fair enough to buy the saved item now, clear alerts when a saved item’s price drops or a sale starts, timely sale / price-drop help so saved items don’t sit until a sale, and proof the product looks like the photos or haul videos. These are what people are missing before a wishlisted product turns into a purchase.

- a price they feel is fair enough to buy the saved item now: 38.6%
- clear alerts when a saved item’s price drops or a sale starts: 11.7%
- timely sale / price-drop help so saved items don’t sit until a sale: 9.0%
- proof the product looks like the photos or haul videos: 7.4%
- Often mentioned, weaker as a wishlist unmet need: hesitate on a saved item because fabric or quality feels uncertain before they commit to a saved item (mentioned #4, conversion rank #12)
